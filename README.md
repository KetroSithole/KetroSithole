## Course Description

Three-plus years spent at the intersection of **software engineering, data engineering, and data science**, applied to one hard problem: how do you build systems that quantify and manage risk *before* it becomes a headline?

This profile is organized like the curriculum I wish I'd had — each module is a discipline I've shipped in production, not just studied. Predictive modeling, backend optimization, and IT risk governance aren't separate tracks here; they're prerequisites for each other.

---

## 🎓 Core Modules

| | Module | What it covers | Instruments |
|---|---|---|---|
| **01** | **Software Engineering Foundations** | Designing backend systems that stay fast and correct as load grows — not just code that runs once | `Python` · `Java` · `SQL` |
| **02** | **Data Engineering & Pipelines** | Moving data reliably at scale: orchestration, streaming, and the unglamorous work of making pipelines fail loudly instead of silently | `PySpark` · `Airflow` · `Kafka` · `Docker` |
| **03** | **Data Science & Predictive Modeling** | Turning historical signal into forward-looking risk models that a business can actually act on | `Python` · `Statistical Analysis` · `ML` |
| **04** | **IT Risk & Governance** | The module most engineers skip: making systems auditable, controlled, and defensible — because a model nobody trusts doesn't get used | Risk Frameworks · Controls Testing |

**Prerequisite chain:** 01 → 02 → 03, with 04 running concurrently throughout. Risk isn't a bolt-on at the end — it's graded into every module.
